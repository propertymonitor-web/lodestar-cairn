CAIRN Field Guide

How to send “I’m OK” and “I need help” when phones do not work.

Quick start — read this first

CAIRN sends short messages from radio to radio. It needs no phone signal, no internet and no licence. Other CAIRN or MeshCore radios (or a repeater) must be within range to hear you.

  1. Antennas on first. Screw the antenna onto each radio before you give it power. A radio that sends with no antenna can be damaged.
  2. Plug in. Plug the tablet, the CAIRN radio (the one with the little screen) and the small repeater radio into the USB hub. Plug a charged power bank into the hub. Wait one minute.
  3. Open the CAIRN page on the tablet. Tap the screen to get past the opening picture. Press Connect radio and choose the radio.
  4. Say you are OK. On the Home screen press I’M OK. Everyone in range sees your tag and where you are.
  5. In trouble? Call 911 first if you can. If you cannot, press I NEED HELP, then YES, SEND HELP. It keeps calling every few minutes until you cancel.
RememberEverything you send, including your position, can be read by anyone with a radio in range. Your tablet and radio need power: check the Power screen now and then.

No tablet? The CAIRN radio has its own small screen. Short press the button to change screens. On the HELP screen, hold the button, then hold it again within 5 seconds to send a help call.

1. What CAIRN is, and what it is not

CAIRN is a pair of small radios and a tablet. It lets people near each other send short text messages, check in, and call for help when phones and the internet are down. It also works on a normal day for hikes, events and camping.

It can
  • Send “I’m OK” with your position
  • Call for help, again and again, until cancelled
  • Send short messages (up to 140 letters)
  • Show who has reported, who needs help, and what is marked on the map
  • Work with no internet at all (load maps ahead of time)
It cannot
  • Call 911 or reach emergency services by itself
  • Reach people who have no radio in range
  • Promise a message arrived
  • Keep a secret on the Public channel

How far it reaches changes a lot. In a city, a few blocks. In the open, or with a repeater on a roof or hill, much farther. Height helps more than anything else: go up, go outside.

2. What is in the kit

Your kit card shows the exact cables. Keep the two radios’ antennas apart from each other and away from the hub and cables: that gives the best range and keeps the Bluetooth link steady.

How the power flows
  • At home or with mains power: plug the hub into the wall charger. Use the charger’s big 85 W port alone; with other ports in use it shares its power.
  • Power is out: power bank 1 takes the charger’s place. It must have a USB-C power-delivery (PD) output, about 30 W or more. Check the bank’s output rating, not just its size.
  • The CAIRN radio runs on its own battery. Charge it from the solar panel or power bank 2. It does not need the hub.
  • Power bank 1 runs low? Swap in the charged one, or recharge it. The tablet and the repeater are off for a little while, and that is fine: a repeater that is off for a short time is far better than no repeater at all. The CAIRN radio keeps working on its own battery, and it still sends its automatic check-ins and help calls to any radio in range.
  • Sunny? Put the solar panel in the sun to top up power bank 2 and the radio. The storm being over does not mean that power, cellular or internet are back, so recharge whenever the sun is out.

3. First-time setup (do this at home, with internet)

  1. Charge everything — tablet, power banks, and the radio’s own battery if it has one.
  2. Open the CAIRN page in Chrome on the tablet. In the browser menu choose Add to Home screen. After that it opens with no internet.
  3. Load a map for your area. Go to Map, pick your region in the list, download it, then press Load a map file. Maps are big, so use Wi-Fi.
  4. Try Practice. At the top, press Practice. The page fills with pretend people so you can press every button safely. Press Stop practice when done.
  5. Check the antenna number. Go to Power. The radio assumes a normal antenna (2 dBi). If you fit a different antenna, type the number printed on it in Antenna gain and press Set. This keeps the radio inside the legal power limit.
  6. Choose where “I’m OK” goes. On Home, These go to channel is normally 0 - Public. Leave it unless your group agreed on another channel.

If your tablet has the CAIRN Station app

The Station app is the same dashboard, built into the tablet, so it starts by itself and does not need Chrome. When it starts it asks for three things, one after another:

  1. Wi-Fi — optional. If you are at home on Wi-Fi, connect it: the Online screen can then fetch weather alerts, earthquake reports, offline maps and updates, and Ask Gemini works. No Wi-Fi? Press Skip; everything else still works.
  2. Repeater — needed. Plug the repeater’s USB cable into the tablet (or its hub), then press Connect beside it and tap Allow when Android asks.
  3. Companion radio — needed. Press Connect beside the radio’s name. The first time, Android asks for a PIN: type the PIN shown on the radio’s own screen. Only one device can hold the radio’s Bluetooth, so close the MeshCore app on any phone first.

If the repeater or the radio disconnects later, these steps come back by themselves until both are connected again. The Files screen holds everything CAIRN has recorded (the journal of what the radio heard, the repeater’s log, saved reports) and can send any of it by email or save it to the tablet’s Download folder. Before a storm: connect Wi-Fi and press Get ready now on the Online screen.

4. Using it: the Home screen

The Home screen is the only one you need in an emergency. Big buttons, plain words.

The colour bar at the top tells you if you can reach people

REACHABLE a repeater is heard. Your messages should get through.
NO REPEATER HEARD go higher or outside; radios right next to you can still hear you.
RADIO NOT CONNECTED plug the radio into the hub and press Connect radio.

The two big buttons

I’M OK Sends your tag (like LC-K7M2P4XQ) and your position. Press it when you arrive somewhere, and every hour or so. Or let the radio do it for you: Power → Automatic I’M OK.

I NEED HELP Asks first (“Send a call for help?”). If you say yes, the radio sends NEEDS HELP with your position, and repeats it every few minutes. The button turns into CANCEL HELP CALL. Cancelling tells everyone you are OK.

Call 911 first when you can.CAIRN only reaches other radios. It does not call emergency services.

Send a status

One tap tells everyone what you need or are doing, with your position:

Need medical Need water Need food Need shelter Need transport Evacuating Staying put Can help

Mark this place

Stand where something is, then tap what it is. It shows on every CAIRN map and in the report:

Water Shelter Medical aid Hazard Meeting point Road blocked Supplies

Ready check

A list of ticks ✓ notes ! and problems ✗. Look at it when you set up and fix the red ones. It ends with READY when all is well.

Situation report

Press Make a report for a short summary of who has reported, who needs help and what is marked. You can read it aloud to a rescuer, copy it, save it, or send a one-line version to everyone.

When someone else calls for help

A big red box appears on every screen with a sound. It shows who, how long ago, and which direction and how far. Press Show on map to see where, and Got it when someone is dealing with it. Turn the sound off with Sound for help calls on Home.

5. Reading what others send

The People screen lists everyone who has reported. It shows NEEDS HELP at the top, their last status, whether their battery is low, and where they are. Under Needs and places you see totals (for example “2 Need water”) and all marked places. Overdue means someone has not been heard from for a while (you can change how long).

The Map shows you, the repeaters, the other people, red pulsing dots for help calls, and orange diamonds for marked places. With no internet, load a map ahead of time (section 3).

6. Chat and channels

Open Chat. Pick a channel on the left, type your message, press Send. Messages are up to 140 letters. Remember that anyone in range may read the Public channel.

What is a channel?

A channel is a group. Everyone who has the same channel can read it, and everyone else cannot (except on Public and Open channels). There are three kinds:

KindWhat it isWho can read it
PublicBuilt in. Channel 0.Everyone with a CAIRN or MeshCore radio nearby
Open (starts with #)Anyone who types the same name joins, for example #water-tankAnyone who knows the name
PrivateNeeds a name and a secret keyOnly people you give the key to

Add or remove a channel

In Chat, open Channels: add or remove (the grey bar under the people list).

  1. Join an open channel: type the name (letters, numbers and dashes) in the first box and press Add.
  2. Make a private channel: type a name and press Make. The radio makes the secret key and shows it. Give the name and key to your people (write it down or press Copy).
  3. Join a private channel: type the name, then the 32-letter key you were given, and press Join.
  4. Remove a channel: press Remove beside it, then Yes, remove. Public cannot be removed. The radio holds up to 40 channels.
Check-ins go to one channelI’m OK, help calls, statuses and marks all go to the channel marked check-ins go here. It starts as Public so everyone nearby sees them. To use a private channel instead, press Use for check-ins beside it. Do this only if everyone who might help you is on that channel.

7. Standard and Disaster mode

STANDARDDISASTER
Use itEvery day, hikes, practiceA real emergency
Radio strengthThe strongest the rules allow. Drops by itself when the battery is low (below 25%).The strongest the rules allow, always, even on a low battery
Radio screenNormalTurns off sooner to save power
Quiet hoursObeyedOff, so nothing is silenced
Silent repeatersMarked stale after a whileMarked stale sooner

Change it with the two buttons on Home, or on the radio’s own POWER screen. The mode is remembered when the radio restarts. A running help call always uses full strength.

Why both modes have the same top powerIn the United States, radios that need no licence have one legal limit (about 1 watt at the antenna, with an antenna up to 6 dBi) and there is no higher limit for emergencies. So Disaster mode does not make the signal stronger than Standard at full power. It makes sure the radio stays at full power and stops it saving battery.

8. Power: batteries, solar, and radio strength

What uses the power

The tablet uses the most (it is like a small lamp). The two radios together use little while listening. Sending a message costs very little. So: turn the tablet screen down, close other apps, and let the tablet sleep. The radios keep working while the tablet sleeps, and the CAIRN radio keeps the log.

The Power screen

  • Radio power and settings — shows the strength now and why. FULL is the strongest allowed; SAVER uses less battery and reaches less far.
  • Battery saving (on by default) — below 25% on battery, Standard mode uses the saver strength and automatic check-ins slow down. It turns itself off at 35% or when you plug power in.
  • Low-battery message — at 15% the radio sends LOW BATTERY once so friends know you may go quiet.
  • How long will my power last? — type in your power bank size, tablet use and solar panel. It tells you roughly how many hours or days you have. It is an estimate, not a promise.
  • Power banks timer — a reminder. The radios cannot see how full a power bank is, so press the button when you start one.

Power banks and solar — simple rules

  1. Charge the power banks before you need them, and top them up whenever you can.
  2. Put the solar panel in direct sun, pointing at the sun, with nothing shading even a corner. Shade on part of a panel cuts its power a lot. Check it every hour or two and turn it toward the sun.
  3. Charge a power bank with the panel; run the kit from a power bank. Charging and running at the same moment works only if your bank allows it.
  4. Keep batteries warm in the cold. Cold makes them lose charge fast. Keep them inside a coat or bag.
  5. Use a hub and cables that can give enough power. The radio draws short bursts of up to about 0.75 amp when it sends at full strength. A weak hub makes the radio restart. If it restarts when sending, change the hub or cable.

Antenna gain and the legal limit

The radio works out its power from the antenna you tell it about. The stock antenna is about 2 dBi. A bigger antenna (higher dBi) sends more strongly in one direction, so the rules make the radio send with less power. If you change the antenna, enter its number on the Power screen.

10. If something is not working

What you seeWhat to do
Cannot press Connect radio, or the radio is not in the listUse Chrome (version 148 or newer on Android). Unplug the radio and plug it in again. Close other apps that use USB. Use a cable that carries data, not a charge-only cable.
NO REPEATER HEARDGo outside, up high, away from metal and walls. Check that the antenna is tight. Wait 10 minutes. Radios near you can still hear you.
Station app: the radio is not in the listMake sure the radio is on and near the tablet, and that no phone is connected to it. Press Find radios. If Android already has an old pairing with it, remove the radio in Android’s Bluetooth settings and connect again.
Station app: the repeater does not showUse a cable that carries data. If two USB devices show, pick the repeater (unplug the radio’s cable, press Refresh, then plug it back in).
Ask Gemini is greyed outGemini needs the internet. Connect Wi-Fi from the first start-up step or Connections at the top.
Buttons are greyThe radio is not connected yet. Press Connect radio.
“No position yet” (no GPS)Go outside or stand by a window and wait a few minutes. Messages still work; they just say no GPS fix.
The radio keeps restarting when sendingThe hub or cable cannot give enough power. Plug the radio into a stronger port or power bank, or use a shorter cable.
Power seems lowSee section 8. Check Battery saving, and that the antenna number is right.
Chat says the person’s key has not been heardWait for their radio to announce itself (it can take several minutes), then try again.
Typing is hardUse the little keyboard button beside a box to bring up the on-screen keyboard, or the Keyboard button at the top.
Everything looks wrongUse the radio’s small reset: unplug it, wait 10 seconds, plug it in. Your channels, mode and settings are remembered.

11. Safety, privacy and the rules

Emergency servicesIf you can use a phone, call 911 (or your local number). CAIRN is a backup. It cannot reach 911.
PrivacyYour tag and position go out to everyone in range when you press I’M OK, a status, a mark, or a help call. Messages on Public and Open channels can be read by anyone. Private channels are for people who have the key.
  • Antennas on first. Never send with no antenna.
  • Do not stand close to the antenna while sending. Keep the antenna a little away from your body and from other people when you are sending a lot.
  • No licence needed in the US. CAIRN is designed to stay inside the limit for unlicensed (FCC Part 15) radios in the 902–928 MHz band. For that to be true, use only the antenna the radio expects (or enter the right number). You may not cause harmful interference, and you must accept interference from others.
  • Outside the US, follow your own country’s rules for this kind of radio, and check the radio’s frequency setting.
  • This is BETA software. It has been through a full code review and tests on a PC, but it has not yet run on real hardware. Practise with it before you rely on it. It is a help, not a guarantee.

14. Words used in this guide

Station tagYour radio’s name tag, like LC-K7M2P4XQ. It is made once and never changes.
RepeaterA radio whose job is to pass messages along so they go farther.
ChannelA group chat. Everyone with the same channel reads it.
KeyA secret code (32 letters and numbers) that opens a private channel.
GPS fixThe radio knows where it is, from satellites. It needs a view of the sky.
dBiHow strongly an antenna focuses the signal. The stock antenna is about 2.
mW / WMilliwatts and watts: how strongly the radio sends. 1000 mW is 1 W.
OverdueSomeone has not been heard from for longer than you set.
